    Selected bars

      1 unit is the work of fully drawing one pixel the normal way on a current NVIDIA card. Path tracing uses 2 bounces, the game's default. Upscaling ratios are from NVIDIA's DLSS Programming Guide (Quality 1.5×, Balanced 1.724×, Performance 2×, Ultra Performance 3× per side; DLAA 1×); AMD FSR uses near-identical ratios. Upscaling is modelled worst case. The upscaler always costs its heaviest model (preset L), and ray traced and path traced bars pay the heavier Ray Reconstruction instead, both from NVIDIA's measured RTX 5070 times (the costliest of the cards here); FSR is assumed to cost the same. Part of every frame doesn't shrink with fewer pixels: 47% at FHD, 41% at 2K and 35% at 4K, fitted to the smallest gains found in published tests (TechSpot's 10-game DLSS Quality average at 1080p, Alan Wake 2 path traced at 4K). Real games usually gain more. No frame generation. Card limits come from published Witcher 3 Remastered results without rays (mainly TechSpot, September 2026), lined up so budget cards handle FHD, middle cards 2K and high-end cards 4K at 60 fps. AMD cards are weaker at path tracing than at normal drawing, so their limit is too generous for the path traced column. Running out of video memory on 8 GB cards at high resolutions isn't modelled. Estimates to show the scale of the work, not measurements.
